Wired earbuds with a USB-C plug are a niche product now, but Apple still makes one — and at $16.91, it's one of the cheapest ways to get decent audio into an iPhone 15 or later, an iPad, or a MacBook without dealing with Bluetooth pairing or charging. What it actually is

This is the USB-C version of Apple's long-running EarPods — the same hard plastic earbud shape that shipped in iPhone boxes for years, now terminated in a USB-C connector instead of Lightning or a 3.5mm jack. You get the earbuds, an inline remote on the right cable with volume up/down and a center button for play/pause, call answering, and track skipping, plus a built-in microphone. That's the whole package. No case, no extra tips, no app.

The sound is what EarPods have always delivered: clear mids and vocals, light bass, and an open fit that lets in ambient noise. For podcasts, calls, and casual music listening, it's perfectly fine. The cable is Apple's standard thin white rubberized type, and the USB-C plug works with iPhone 15 and newer, recent iPads, Macs, and most Android phones and laptops with USB-C audio support.

Who it's for

The obvious buyer is someone with a USB-C iPhone or iPad who wants a cheap, reliable backup pair of earbuds — the kind you keep in a bag, a desk drawer, or a car glovebox and don't worry about losing. At under $17, replacing them doesn't hurt.

They're also a good fit for people who dislike in-ear silicone tips. The EarPods' hard shell rests in the outer ear rather than sealing the canal, which some people find far more comfortable for long calls or all-day wear. Students, office workers on back-to-back video calls, and anyone who wants zero latency for video or gaming without pairing hassles will get their money's worth.

Where it falls short

The open design is also the biggest weakness. There's no noise isolation at all, so on a plane, train, or busy street you'll be cranking the volume to compete with background noise — and people next to you will hear some of your audio leaking out. Bass response is thin compared to any sealed in-ear earbud at this price.

The fit is the other gamble. EarPods either sit well in your ears or they don't, and since there are no tip sizes to swap, there's no fix if your ears aren't the right shape. They can work loose during exercise. The cable also tangles easily, and Apple's thin cables have a long history of fraying near the connectors if you wrap them tightly.

What to check before buying

  • Your device's port. These are USB-C only. They won't fit older Lightning iPhones, and they won't work in a 3.5mm headphone jack. If you have an iPhone 14 or earlier, you need the Lightning version instead.
  • Android compatibility. Most modern Android phones handle USB-C audio fine, but a few budget models don't output audio over USB-C. Check your phone's specs if you're not buying for an Apple device.
  • Your ear shape. If you've tried EarPods before and they fell out, these will too. The shape hasn't changed.
  • How you'll use them. If you need isolation for commuting or bass for music, a sealed in-ear option — even a cheap wired one — will serve you better. If you want something for calls, podcasts, and a desk drawer, these are hard to beat at this price.
