The Amazon Fire TV Stick HD is a small streaming dongle that plugs directly into your TV's HDMI port and turns any television into a smart TV. At $39.99, it sits at the budget end of Amazon's streaming lineup, and it comes with the Alexa Voice Remote in the box, so you can search for shows and control playback by speaking instead of typing with arrow keys.

Setup is about as simple as it gets: plug the stick into an HDMI port, connect the power cable, join your Wi-Fi, and sign into your Amazon account. The remote pairs automatically. From there you get access to the major streaming apps — Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+, Hulu, YouTube — plus a selection of free and live TV options through ad-supported services, which is a genuine selling point if you're trying to cut subscription costs. It holds a 4.6 out of 5 rating, which reflects how reliably it does this basic job.

Who it's for

This stick makes the most sense for three types of buyers. First, anyone with an older "dumb" TV that works fine but has no smart features — this is a cheap way to add streaming without replacing the television. Second, people whose smart TV has a slow or clunky interface; a $40 stick is often faster and better supported than the software built into budget TVs. Third, heavy Amazon ecosystem users — if you have Prime, Echo speakers, or Ring cameras, the Alexa integration ties things together neatly.

It's also a reasonable pick for a guest room, a dorm, or a second TV where you don't want to spend much. At this price, it's close to an impulse purchase, and replacement cost is low if it ever fails.

Where it falls short

The "HD" in the name is the key limitation: this model tops out at 1080p. If you have a 4K TV — and most TVs sold in the last several years are 4K — you won't be getting the full picture quality your screen can deliver. For a primary living room TV, the 4K version of the Fire TV Stick is usually the smarter buy, even though it costs more.

The other trade-off is the interface itself. Amazon's Fire TV home screen is heavy on promoted content and ads, and it pushes Prime Video hard. It works fine, but it's not a neutral experience. Performance is also adequate rather than snappy — budget sticks can lag a bit when navigating menus, and this is a budget stick. Finally, because it's powered by a cable rather than the TV's HDMI port alone, you'll need a free USB port on the TV or a wall outlet nearby, which slightly complicates a clean wall-mounted setup.

What to check before buying

  • Your TV's resolution. If it's a 4K set, strongly consider spending a bit more for the 4K model instead of capping yourself at 1080p.
  • A spare HDMI port and power source. Confirm you have an open HDMI input plus either a USB port on the TV or an outlet within cable reach.
  • Wi-Fi strength where the TV sits. There's no Ethernet port on a stick this size, so weak Wi-Fi in that room means buffering.
  • The apps you actually use. Check that your must-have services are available on Fire TV — the big ones all are, but niche apps occasionally aren't.

If your TV is 1080p or smaller, your Wi-Fi is solid, and you just want streaming that works without fuss, this is a sensible $40 spend.
