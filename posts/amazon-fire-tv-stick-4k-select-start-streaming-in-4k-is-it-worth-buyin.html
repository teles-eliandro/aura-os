If your TV already has a smart platform you hate, or no smart platform at all, a streaming stick is the cheapest fix. The Amazon Fire TV Stick 4K Select sits at the budget end of Amazon's 4K lineup at $49.99, and it does the core job well — with a few compromises you should know about before checkout.

What it actually is

The 4K Select is a small HDMI dongle that plugs into the back of your TV and hides behind it. It streams in 4K resolution with HDR support, connects over Wi-Fi, and comes with Amazon's Alexa voice remote, so you can search for shows by speaking instead of pecking at an on-screen keyboard. Amazon has been pushing its AI-powered search, which lets you ask for things in natural language — "comedies from the 90s" rather than exact titles — and it works reasonably well for surfacing stuff across apps.

Setup is simple: plug it in, connect to Wi-Fi, sign into your Amazon account, and install your apps. You get access to the major services — Netflix, Prime Video, Disney+, YouTube, Max and the rest — plus a decent amount of free, ad-supported content through Amazon's own channels and apps like Tubi and Pluto TV. The "free & live TV" angle in the product name refers to that ad-supported content, not cable channels, so set expectations accordingly.

Who it's for

This is the right buy for someone with a 4K TV who wants streaming for under $50 and doesn't care about the extras on pricier models. It's a good fit for a bedroom or guest-room TV, for a first apartment, or for anyone upgrading an older 1080p Fire Stick who now owns a 4K set.

It also makes sense if your household is already in Amazon's world — Prime membership, Alexa speakers, Ring doorbells. The remote ties into that ecosystem neatly, and Prime Video is front and center in the interface.

If you're a heavy tinkerer who sideloads apps, or a home theater enthusiast chasing the best possible audio passthrough and the fastest processor, this isn't the model for you. Look higher up the range or at competitors.

Where it falls short

The biggest trade-off is the operating system. The 4K Select runs Amazon's newer Vega OS rather than the Android-based Fire OS used on older sticks. In practice that means a smaller app catalog at launch and, importantly, no easy sideloading of third-party apps — something many longtime Fire Stick users relied on. If you have a niche app in mind, verify it's available before buying.

Second, the interface is ad-heavy. Amazon promotes its own content and sponsored rows aggressively on the home screen, and there's no way to turn that off completely. Performance is fine for everyday streaming, but this is the entry-level 4K model, so don't expect the snappiest navigation when scrolling through large libraries. The 4.1/5 rating — noticeably lower than some rival streamers — reflects some of these frustrations, particularly around the OS change.

Finally, like all sticks in this class, it depends entirely on your Wi-Fi. There's no Ethernet port, so a weak signal in the room means buffering no matter what you buy.

What to check before buying

  • Your must-have apps. Because of the newer OS, confirm every service you actually use has a native app available for this model.
  • Wi-Fi strength where the TV lives. If the signal is weak, budget for a mesh node or consider a streamer with Ethernet support instead.
  • Whether you need Dolby Vision or advanced audio formats. Check the spec sheet against your TV and soundbar — the entry model may not match what your setup can handle.
  • Your tolerance for ads on the home screen. If a clean interface matters to you, compare against Roku or Apple TV before committing.

At $49.99, the Fire TV Stick 4K Select is a sensible, low-risk way to get 4K streaming onto a TV — as long as you go in knowing it's the basic model, not the flagship.
