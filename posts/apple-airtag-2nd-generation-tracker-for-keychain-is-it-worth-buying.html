If you've ever torn apart your couch cushions looking for keys, a Bluetooth tracker is the obvious fix — and for iPhone owners, the AirTag is the default choice. The 2nd generation model keeps the same basic idea: a small, coin-sized disc you attach to a keychain, drop in a wallet, or tuck into a bag, then locate through Apple's Find My app.

What it actually is

The AirTag is a small tracking disc, roughly the size of a large coin, that pairs with your iPhone. Setup is genuinely simple — hold it near your phone, tap once, and it's linked to your Apple ID. From there, the Find My app shows its location on a map, and you can trigger a built-in speaker to play a sound when the item is nearby but out of sight.

What makes AirTags more useful than cheaper Bluetooth trackers is Apple's Find My network. When your tagged item is out of your phone's Bluetooth range, any passing iPhone can anonymously relay its location back to you. In practice, that means a lost bag in a city has a decent chance of showing up on the map. At $22.56 for a single tag, it sits in the middle of the tracker market — not the cheapest, but far from expensive. The 4.5/5 rating reflects what most buyers find: it does one job and does it reliably.

Who it's for

This is for iPhone users, full stop. If you carry an iPhone and regularly misplace keys, a wallet, a backpack, or luggage, one or two AirTags will pay for themselves in saved time the first month. It's also a sensible buy for travelers who want a rough location on checked luggage, and for parents who want to keep track of a kid's backpack.

If your household is split between iPhone and Android, or you want to track something that moves fast and far (like a pet off-leash in open country), this is the wrong tool.

Where it falls short

The biggest limitation is the Apple ecosystem lock-in. AirTags don't work with Android phones at all — an Android user can't even set one up. If you switch platforms, your tags become paperweights.

Second, there's no built-in keyring hole. To attach it to keys you need a separate holder or loop, which typically adds $10 or more to the real cost. The speaker is also quiet enough that it can be hard to hear if the tag is buried under a pile of laundry or inside a padded bag. And while the Find My network is excellent in populated areas, coverage thins out in rural places where few iPhones pass by.

What to check before buying

  • Your phone: Confirm you have an iPhone running a recent version of iOS. Without it, the AirTag is useless.
  • How many you need: Singles cost more per tag than multi-packs. If you're tagging keys, a wallet, and a bag, price out a 4-pack before buying individually.
  • Accessories: Budget for a keyring holder or adhesive mount — the bare disc has no attachment point.
  • Precision Finding: The close-range directional finding feature requires a newer iPhone with a U1 or later chip. Check your model if that feature matters to you; on older iPhones you still get map location and sound, just not the on-screen arrows.
